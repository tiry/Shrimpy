# Phosphate

What it is and what moves it. Current values are `aqua status`; what to do about a flag is
`references/triage.md`.

## What it is

A dissolved nutrient, arriving from food, waste and decomposition, and — like nitrate —
with **no removal path in a normal freshwater tank except export and uptake.** It is not
directly toxic to fish or invertebrates at any concentration an aquarium reaches.

Its significance is as a **growth limiter**. In enclosed water, plant and algal growth is
typically limited by whichever nutrient runs out first, and phosphate is a common candidate.
A rising phosphate alongside available light is the standard precondition for an algae
bloom, and `assets/wiki/pest/cyanobacteria.md` covers the case where the organism involved
is not an alga at all and starving it of nitrate does not work.

A low reading is not automatically good news: it can mean something is consuming it quickly,
which is the same information as a bloom.

## What makes it rise

- **Feeding**, the dominant source. Prepared foods are phosphate-rich and uneaten food more
  so.
- **Decomposition** of waste, dead plant matter or a dead animal.
- **Some fertilisers and buffers**, and occasionally tap water, which can carry a meaningful
  concentration before it reaches the tank.
- **Evaporation with top-off**, concentrating what is present.

## How it comes down

- **Water changes**, the same lever as nitrate and for the same reason.
- **Plant uptake**, growing and then physically removing plant mass.
- **Feeding less**, and removing what is not eaten rather than leaving it to break down.
- **Binding media** designed for the purpose, which is a targeted rather than a general fix.

## What not to do

**Do not chase a number.** Phosphate is worth tracking as a trend and against algae, not as
a target to hit. Aggressive removal can leave plants nutrient-starved while the algae, which
are better at scavenging, continue.

**Do not test it with a strip** when it matters. The resolution is poor and the reading is a
screen — see `assets/wiki/method/test-strip.md`.

**Do not read it in isolation.** Phosphate rising with nitrate is an export problem; rising
alone points at a source, most often the food.
