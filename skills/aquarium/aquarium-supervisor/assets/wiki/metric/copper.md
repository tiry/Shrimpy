# Copper

**There is no reason to raise this, ever, in a tank with invertebrates in it.** The target
is zero. This page is about how it gets in and how to keep it out. What the tanks read is
`aqua status`; what to do about a positive reading is `references/triage.md`, urgently.

## Why it matters more here than in a fish tank

Crustaceans and molluscs carry oxygen with **haemocyanin**, a copper-based protein, rather
than the iron-based haemoglobin vertebrates use. Copper is therefore structural to their
physiology rather than incidental, and their tolerance for dissolved copper is far lower
than a fish's — `reference.json` records the concentration at which it becomes toxic to
Neocaridina, in the low tens of parts per **billion**. That is a level many test methods
cannot resolve at all.

The practical consequence: **a dose calculated as safe for fish can be lethal to every
shrimp and snail in the tank**, and the most common source is a product bought to help.

## How it gets in

- **Fish medications.** Many treatments for external parasites are copper-based; white spot
  remedies are the classic case. See `assets/wiki/pest/ich.md`.
- **Algaecides and some snail treatments**, deliberately.
- **Plant fertilisers**, where copper appears as a trace micronutrient. The quantity is tiny
  by design and still worth checking against a shrimp tank.
- **Plumbing.** Water standing in copper pipes picks it up, so the first draw from a tap can
  carry more than a flushed one.
- **Decor and equipment** containing brass or bronze.

## How to remove it

Poorly, is the honest answer, and that is the reason for the caution above.

- **A conditioner binds heavy metals**, which protects the animals from what is dissolved
  now — see `assets/wiki/product/prime.md`.
- **Activated carbon** adsorbs some of it.
- **Repeated large water changes.**

But copper **adsorbs onto substrate, decor and silicone and leaches back out over a long
period**. A tank that has been treated with a copper medication is not reliably safe for
invertebrates again, and no water change fully reverses it. This is a decision about the
tank's future, not about this week.

## What not to do

**Do not put a product in the tank without checking it for copper first**, including ones
that do not advertise it and ones marketed as gentle or natural.

**Do not rely on a test strip** to clear a product. The resolution is nowhere near the
concentrations that matter — see `assets/wiki/method/test-strip.md`. Read the label.

**Do not treat fish for parasites in a tank holding shrimp or snails.** Treat them
elsewhere.
