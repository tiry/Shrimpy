# Pomacea diffusa

Background only. Run `aqua species mystery_snail` for what it needs, `aqua livestock` for
what is in the tanks.

## What it is

The spike-topped apple snail, a freshwater gastropod of the family Ampullariidae, native to
the Amazon basin and sold universally as the "mystery snail". It reaches golf-ball size and
comes in colour forms — gold, ivory, blue, magenta — that are shell pigmentation, not
different species.

It is routinely confused with *Pomacea canaliculata*, the channelled apple snail, which is a
serious agricultural pest and a destroyer of aquatic plants. **That confusion is worth
undoing carefully, because the two behave completely differently:** *P. diffusa* is a
detritivore and algae grazer that leaves healthy plants alone, while *P. canaliculata* eats
them. Import restrictions that exist because of the latter are frequently quoted at keepers
of the former.

A mystery snail on a plant is almost always eating the decaying part. If it is stripping
healthy leaves, it is underfed.

## Two things that make it unusual

**It breathes air.** Ampullariids have both a gill and a lung, and extend a long siphon to
the surface to draw air. Two practical consequences: the tank needs an air gap above the
water, because a snail that cannot reach the surface will eventually drown; and a snail
stretching up to the surface is behaving normally, not escaping bad water.

**It lays eggs above the waterline.** Clutches go on the glass or the cover in the air,
which makes them trivially easy to see and to remove if they are not wanted. This is the
opposite of most aquarium snails and is the reason a mystery snail population does not
explode unnoticed — unlike bladder or ramshorn snails, whose eggs are submerged and easily
missed.

## The shell is the health record

Shell condition is the most useful indicator this animal offers, and it is visible without
testing anything.

- **Pitting, thinning or a chalky white eroded look** means the shell is dissolving faster
  than it is being built — a dissolved-mineral or pH problem. `references/chemistry.md` has
  the mechanism.
- **A clean new band at the opening** means active, healthy growth.
- **A crack or a chip** heals if the animal is otherwise well; the repair shows as a visible
  seam.

Shell damage is not cosmetic. The shell is skeleton and armour, and a snail cannot repair it
without the minerals to do so.

## With shrimp, and the treatment conflict

Entirely compatible — no predation in either direction, and both are grazers working the
same surfaces.

The conflict is chemical, not behavioural, and it is the single most important thing to know
before medicating: **the standard treatments for planaria and hydra are also snail
killers.** Anything sold as safe for shrimp is very often deliberately lethal to snails.
Before any such product is opened, see `assets/wiki/pest/planaria.md`.

The same caution covers copper, which is toxic to both. Check every medication and plant
fertiliser.
