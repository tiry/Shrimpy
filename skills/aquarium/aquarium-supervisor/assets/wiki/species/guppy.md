# Poecilia reticulata

Background only. What they need and what the tank reads are `aqua species guppy` and
`aqua status`.

## What it is

A small livebearing fish of the family Poeciliidae, native to north-eastern South America
and now introduced almost everywhere warm — often deliberately, for mosquito control, which
is where "millionfish" comes from. It is one of the most studied vertebrates in evolutionary
biology precisely because it breeds fast and varies visibly.

Females are noticeably larger with a plain body and a fan-shaped anal fin; males are small,
slender and carry the colour, with the anal fin modified into a gonopodium — a rod used to
transfer sperm. The difference is unmistakable once the fish are mature and is the only
sexing anyone needs.

## Livebearing, and why it dominates everything

Guppies do not lay eggs. Young develop internally and are released free-swimming, fully
formed, and able to eat immediately. Two consequences run the tank:

- **Females store sperm.** A single mating fertilises several successive broods over
  months. A female separated from every male will still produce young repeatedly, so
  "removing the males" does not stop reproduction and is a common surprise.
- **The population compounds.** Broods arrive on a cycle of weeks, and the young mature
  quickly enough to breed within the same season. A mixed-sex group does not reach a stable
  number on its own; it reaches the tank's carrying capacity.

Adults eat their own fry, which is the only thing keeping the numbers in check in a bare
tank. Dense planting or moss changes that balance sharply — cover means far more survivors,
and a heavily planted tank fills faster than anyone expects.

## Living with shrimp

Guppies are not predators of adult shrimp. They are opportunists with small mouths, and the
honest answer is one of scale rather than aggression:

- An adult shrimp is far too large to be eaten and is generally ignored.
- **Newly hatched shrimplets are the right size to be eaten**, and will be, by any fish
  that finds them.

So a mixed tank is stable for the adults and hostile to recruitment. A shrimp colony sharing
with guppies does not usually collapse; it stops growing, which looks like nothing happening
and is easy to misread as a water-quality problem. Cover is the mitigation — dense moss and
leaf litter give shrimplets somewhere a guppy cannot follow.

If a colony has stopped producing juveniles, check what else is in the tank before checking
the chemistry. `assets/wiki/pest/planaria.md` covers the other common cause of the same
symptom.

## What to watch

Selective breeding for finnage and colour has left ornamental strains less robust than the
wild type — long fins are vulnerable to tearing and to fin rot, and heavily line-bred stock
is more prone to disease on arrival than its hardiness reputation suggests. The species is
tolerant; a given fancy guppy may not be.

`references/triage.md` covers what to do about a sick fish. This page only explains why the
robust reputation and the fragile individual are both true.
