# Otocinclus

Background only. Run `aqua species otocinclus` for what they need and `aqua livestock` for
what is actually in the tanks.

## What it is

A genus of small South American armoured catfish in the family Loricariidae — the
suckermouth catfishes — reaching only a few centimetres. They are grazers, holding onto
glass, leaves and hardscape with a sucker mouth and rasping at the film that grows there.

Unlike most of their family they stay genuinely small, which is why they are one of the few
suckermouth catfish suited to a modest tank. They are also unusual in being almost entirely
harmless: no territoriality worth the name, no aggression, no interest in other animals.

## The thing that actually kills them

**Starvation, usually weeks after purchase, usually blamed on something else.**

Otocinclus eat biofilm and soft algae, continuously, in quantity. They are not equipped to
switch to prepared food the way most community fish are, and a tank clean enough to look
well kept may not support them at all. The sequence is consistent and worth recognising
early: they arrive, graze the existing film down over a week or two, then slowly lose
condition — the belly goes from rounded to flat to concave — and die a month later in
water that tests perfectly.

Three things follow:

- **Only add them to a mature tank.** A new setup has no standing film, and there is nothing
  to graze while the tank establishes.
- **Watch the belly, not the behaviour.** A rounded abdomen means a feeding fish. A flat or
  sunken one is the warning, and it appears well before any change in activity.
- **Supplement deliberately.** Blanched vegetables and a surface that is allowed to grow
  film are what keep them going once the initial algae is gone.

A second, separate problem compounds it: most stock is wild-caught and arrives already
depleted from collection and transport. Losses in the first fortnight are common and are not
usually about the receiving tank.

## Shoaling

They are social and do badly alone. A single individual hides, grazes less and is more
prone to the decline above. `aqua species otocinclus` records the shoal minimum this system
uses; keeping fewer than that is not a preference but a welfare problem.

## With shrimp

Excellent companions and one of the genuinely safe choices. They have no mouth parts for
taking prey, no interest in shrimplets, and they compete only for the biofilm — which is
worth noting, because in a small tank an otocinclus shoal and a shrimp colony are eating
the same thing. See `assets/wiki/species/neocaridina.md` for the shrimp side of that diet.

An otocinclus on a dead or dying shrimp is grazing it, not hunting. The same misreading
applies to shrimp on a dying plant.
