# Scutariella japonica

Background only. `references/triage.md` owns what to do about it.

**No Wikipedia article covers this species directly.** The family-level article on
Temnocephalida is the nearest sourced description, and the husbandry detail below comes
from the aquarium literature rather than from a citable encyclopedic source — it is flagged
rather than dressed up.

## What it is

A tiny flatworm — a temnocephalid, an ectosymbiotic group that lives attached to freshwater
crustaceans — found on dwarf shrimp. On an affected animal it shows as small white threads,
usually on the rostrum, around the head, or at the gill covers, moving slightly. Individual
worms are around a millimetre or two.

They arrive on new shrimp, essentially always. A tank that has never had shrimp added does
not develop them spontaneously.

## The distinction that matters

**Scutariella is on the shrimp. Planaria are on the glass.**

That single sentence resolves most of the confusion, and getting it wrong is expensive,
because the treatments differ completely and the planaria treatment is the one that kills
snails. See `assets/wiki/pest/planaria.md`.

| | Scutariella | Planaria |
|---|---|---|
| Where | attached to the shrimp, head and gills | gliding on glass, substrate, hardscape |
| Size | around a millimetre or two | millimetres to a couple of centimetres |
| Head | no distinct shape | blunt arrow with two eyespots |
| Threat | irritation; heavy loads affect gills | predation on shrimplets and eggs |

## How serious it is

Less serious than it looks, which is worth saying plainly because a first sighting is
alarming. They feed on the shrimp's mucus and on particles in the passing water rather than
on the shrimp's tissue, so a light load is tolerated well and many colonies carry one
indefinitely without visible harm.

The concern is a heavy infestation, where worms clustering at the gills interfere with
respiration, and affected shrimp become lethargic and stop feeding. Egg capsules are laid on
the shrimp's carapace and are lost at the next molt, which is why the condition waxes and
wanes with the molt cycle.

## Why it shows up when it does

An established load often becomes visible after the shrimp are already stressed. Both the
worm's population and the keeper's attention rise when something else is wrong, so
Scutariella is frequently a symptom of a tank problem rather than the cause of one.

Treat the finding as a prompt to check the water and the recent history — `aqua status`
and `aqua questions` — rather than as an isolated parasite to be eliminated.
